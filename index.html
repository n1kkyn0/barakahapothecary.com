OUR STORY

Named for the seed of blessing.

For centuries, the black seed has been known in Arabic as habbat al-barakah—the “seed of blessing.” Long before modern herbalism, this tiny seed was revered across the ancient world, treasured from the markets of the Middle East to the apothecaries of Spain’s Al-Andalus era for its remarkable properties and traditional healing heritage.

Because of this rich history of reverence and use, it came to be known as a gift of wellness—a tradition of care carried forward through generations.

Barakah Apothecary began with that seed, and our first line, Barakah Balm, carries its name. Our flagship Black Seed blend folds black seed oil into whipped tallow.

We make every jar in small batches, with simple, wholesome ingredients we would gladly put on our own children’s skin. For a balm that is mostly tallow, the quality of that tallow is everything.

That’s why we insist on tallow that is both grass-fed and grass-finished. “Grass-fed” alone can mean cattle that started on pasture but spent their final months eating grain. Grass-finished means pasture from start to finish, and that shows up in the fat itself: a richer, more golden tallow with a different natural makeup than grain-finished fat.

When we went looking for a balm that was both halal and grass-finished, we couldn’t find one. The few halal tallow balms we came across used grass-fed tallow, but not grass-finished. So we made our own, with nothing to compromise on.

HOW TO USE

A little goes a long way.

Tallow balm is richer than a lotion. Start small, and let the warmth of your hands do the work.

For external use only. Stop using it if irritation develops.

  1. 1

    Patch test first

    Dab a little on the inside of your forearm and wait 24 hours before using it more widely.

  2. 2

    Take a little

    A pea-sized scoop goes a long way. Tallow balm is rich.

  3. 3

    Warm it between your fingertips

    It softens in seconds. Press it into slightly damp skin rather than rubbing hard.

  4. 4

    Store it cool and dark

    Below about 75°F it stays whipped. If it softens in the heat, a few minutes in the fridge firms it up again.
